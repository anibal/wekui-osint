TWO QUESTIONS, IN THIS ORDER. They are not the same question, and the verdict is which one fails.

FIRST: do the posts satisfy the rule above? Read the rule, not the theme's name. Most of this corpus is relatives ASKING, not reporters TELLING, and a rule that needs an assertion is not met by a question, however grave. «¿Alguien tiene información sobre residencias Albatros?» asks; it asserts nothing about anyone's whereabouts. If the rule is not met — "unsupported". The happening did not occur as far as this evidence goes.
- A LIST IS AN ASSERTION. A post listing «Edificio Bellevue, Residencias Miramar, Hotel Catimar» under collapsed buildings asserts that each one collapsed, as fully as a sentence would. Do not flag a claim because its evidence is terse.
- A COUNT IS NOT A NAME. «12 edificios caídos», «DOS edificios de la Misión Vivienda» and «hay personas atrapadas» all assert that something happened, but a rule needing a specific NAMED structure or person is not met by a number — and the name of a programme, a mission or a neighbourhood is not the name of a building. A list names each one; a count names none.
- APPLY THE RULE AS WRITTEN, INCLUDING ITS PERMISSIVE WORDS. A rule saying a person is «believed OR shown» alive is met by belief; a rule saying «sent, deployed, OR has personnel present» is met by presence. A person weighed those words and chose them. Reading a ratified rule more strictly than it is written is not caution — it is overruling it, and it deletes true claims.
- AN ACTIVITY IS NOT ITS OUTCOME, and doubt about the outcome does not unmake the activity. «Continúan las labores de rescate… sin embargo, no hay confirmación de que haya personas con vida» ASSERTS that the operation is under way; the doubt is about who will be found. When the rule asks whether teams are working, that rule is met. Only a rule asking for the outcome itself is defeated by an unconfirmed outcome.

SECOND, only if the rule IS met: do the posts bear the rest of the claim, at the strength it states — the person and their details, the place, the numbers, the status? If they bear the happening but the claim says more than they do — a floor, a toll, a certainty, a status the posts never gave — "overstated". If they bear all of it — "supported".

So: "unsupported" means the wrong thing, or nothing, happened. "overstated" means the right thing happened and the claim inflated it. A claim can only be overstated if it cleared the first question.

A HEDGE DOES NOT FAIL THE RULE — IT CAPS THE STRENGTH, AND THAT IS WHAT "overstated" IS FOR. Use all three verdicts; a gate that only ever answers two has stopped reading one of them. The line runs like this:
- «Posible señales de vida en la OPP22» — signs WERE detected, and the rule names detected signs outright. The rule is met. What the claim dropped is the word «posible». That is "overstated".
- «Rescatar personas que puedan estar aún vivas» — no contact, no sound, no movement, only hope. Nothing the rule asks for is present at all. That is "unsupported".
- A post that asserts one person flatly and adds «posiblemente su mamá también» supports a claim about the first and OVERSTATES one about the second.
Evidence present but hedged is "overstated". Evidence absent is "unsupported". Do not collapse the two.

READ PAST THE OPENING WORDS, IN BOTH DIRECTIONS. A post very often asks AND tells, and its first phrase is the trap. «Se solicita información sobre EZIO NARDUCCI, donde perdieron comunicación tras el terremoto» opens as a request and then ASSERTS that contact was lost — a claim of a missing person is supported here, and flagging it because it begins "se solicita" would erase a real one. The opposite trap is the same trap: a post that only asks does not become an assertion because it sounds desperate.

UNCONFIRMED IS NOT CONFIRMED. The test is grammatical. When the post puts the event in the conditional or reportative — «habría colapsado», «presuntamente», «dicen que», «no está confirmado» — it evidences that a report circulated, not that the thing happened. A claim stating it outright is at best "overstated"; a claim resting on nothing else is "unsupported".
- BUT ATTRIBUTION IS NOT HEDGING. «El USGS reporta un sismo de 4.6 al norte de Caraballeda» and «the U.S. Geological Survey said the tremor struck at a depth of 10 kilometres» name WHO says it, on the record. That is the strongest evidence this corpus offers, not the weakest — the opposite of «dicen que», which names nobody. And where the rule is about an institution stating or announcing something, the attribution IS the happening the rule asks for. Language is irrelevant: a post in English, Turkish or Spanish evidences the same fact.
