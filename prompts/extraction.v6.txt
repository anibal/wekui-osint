WHAT IS A HAPPENING. A claim is one discrete event — something that OCCURRED at a moment you could point to: someone trapped, rescued, missing, or found dead; a building or a bridge that came down; an official toll announced; aid that arrived; a neighbour's plea from a specific place. Weigh the material:
- Many posts about the same event are ONE claim that cites them all — volume is not novelty.
- Different people or different events are different claims, even when the words are nearly identical — tell them apart by their specifics, and never merge them on a shared template.
- A single post may carry several events, or none — a missing-person post that also says their building fell holds two.
- A standing condition, a general description, a risk or a warning, an explanation, an opinion, an appeal for prayers, a promotion is NOT an event — however grave it sounds. "The area was upscale", "the rain could weaken the rubble", "everything is destroyed" describe or warn; nothing occurred at a moment. For each candidate ask: what happened, and when? If you cannot name both, there is no claim. Silence is the honest answer.

A QUESTION IS NOT A REPORT. Most of this corpus is relatives asking, not reporters telling — and the difference is the whole of a person's dignity. "Se busca a X", "está desaparecida", "no sabemos de su paradero" ASSERT that someone is missing: that is a claim, and its status is desaparecido. "¿Saben algo de ella?", "se solicita información sobre X", "tengo familiares en ese edificio", "cualquier información sobre el estado de las residencias Y" ASK. A question is evidence that someone is looking; it is NOT evidence that the person is missing, trapped or dead. Record what the post asserts and no more:
- A plea that names a person and says where they were takes status null, never desaparecido, unless the post itself says they are missing or unreachable.
- A plea that asks only about a BUILDING, with no person named and no one said to be inside, is not a claim about a person at all — and usually not a claim about anything. Do not invent "una persona" to fill the subject.
- Never write a status the post does not state. An absent status is honest; an assumed one is a false report about a real family.

UNCONFIRMED IS NOT CONFIRMED. When a post marks its own content as unverified — "no es nada confirmado", "dicen que", "según algunas publicaciones", "me dijeron que", "al parecer" — it does not evidence that the thing happened. Either record nothing, or record it as what it is: a report circulating, with the uncertainty stated in "subject_role" ("vecinos reportan…") and a low confidence. A building named inside such a post has NOT collapsed as far as this record is concerned, and two buildings named there are not two collapses.

THE PEOPLE. This memorial is drawn from public posts and exists to connect people with real human stories, so we honour the people in them and protect their dignity. In the description a reader sees, there is no name: not the person's, not a relative's, and not a public figure invoked to point at them — "the father of pianist Gabriela Montero" identifies a private man, so write "a man". A child is only ever a role, never a name, even when a post names them. Public officials and organizations acting publicly — a president, a rescue brigade, a ministry — are the source that reports a happening, not the private person it is about, so they do not belong in the description or in "names".
