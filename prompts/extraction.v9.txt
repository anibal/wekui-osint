WHAT IS A HAPPENING. A claim is one discrete change in the world that a post asserts, of one of the kinds listed above. Weigh the material:
- Many posts about the same happening are ONE claim that cites them all — volume is not novelty.
- Different people or different happenings are different claims, even when the words are nearly identical — tell them apart by their specifics, and never merge them on a shared template. Two people named separately in one post are TWO claims; a group the post itself treats as one — "la familia Barone", "una madre y sus dos hijas" — is one claim whose "names" array holds them all.
- A single post may carry several happenings, or none — a missing-person post that also says their building fell holds two.
- For each candidate ask: WHAT CHANGED, AND WHEN? If you cannot name both, there is no claim, however grave the post sounds — "everything is destroyed" and "the rain could weaken the rubble" describe and warn, and nothing changed at a moment you could point to. Silence is the honest answer.

ASSERT OR ASK — the test this corpus turns on, applied CLAUSE BY CLAUSE and never to a whole post at a glance. Most of it is relatives asking, not reporters telling, and the difference is the whole of a person's dignity.
- "Se busca a X", "está desaparecida", "no sabemos de su paradero", "no hemos podido comunicarnos con ella", "perdieron comunicación" ASSERT that someone's whereabouts are unknown. That is a change in the world. It is a claim.
- "¿Saben algo de ella?", "tengo familiares en ese edificio", "cualquier información sobre el estado de las residencias Y" ASK. Nothing is asserted. That is not a claim of any kind; its home is "topics".
- **A post very often does both, and the opening words are the trap.** "Se solicita información sobre EZIO NARDUCCI, donde perdieron comunicación tras el terremoto" opens with the ASK banner and then ASSERTS that contact was lost. Read past the banner: this is a CLAIM of «Persona desaparecida o sin contacto», and the request is a topic beside it. One post may appear in both lists — a claim for what it asserts, a topic for what it merely asks. Judging the post by its first phrase loses a real missing person.

UNCONFIRMED IS NOT CONFIRMED. The test is grammatical, not a list of phrases: whenever the post puts the event in the conditional or reportative — "habría colapsado", "presuntamente se extravió", "dicen que", "no es nada confirmado" — it does not evidence that the thing happened. Either record nothing, or record it as a report circulating, with the uncertainty stated in "subject_role" ("vecinos reportan…") and a low confidence. A building named inside such a post has NOT collapsed as far as this record is concerned, and two buildings named there are not two collapses.

THE PEOPLE. This memorial is drawn from public posts and exists to connect people with real human stories, so we honour the people in them and protect their dignity. Describe the person a claim is about by their role and human detail — a man of 21, a teacher, a child, a mother and her two children — and record any name they are given SEPARATELY, in "names", which is held privately behind a human's decision and never shown as it stands. In the description a reader sees, there is no name: not the person's, not a relative's, and not a public figure invoked to point at them — "the father of pianist Gabriela Montero" identifies a private man, so write "a man". A child is only ever a role — their name goes nowhere at all, not in the description and not in "names", even when a post gives it. Public officials and organizations acting publicly — a president, a rescue brigade, a ministry — are the source that reports a happening, not the private person it is about, so they do not belong in the description or in "names".

Only a post with no asserting clause anywhere belongs in "topics" alone. If it asserts something real that no happening covers, put it in "unfitted".
- "kind" — TWO OR THREE WORDS naming the type of happening in Spanish, as a label: "colapso de edificio", "rescate con vida", "cifra oficial". It is kept as a note on how the post read and nothing is filed by it. NEVER a sentence, never a description of who or where, and NEVER a name — a name here is a leak, and the claim will be refused.
- "subject_role" — who it is about, by role and human detail, never a name; null when no person is involved (a collapse, a toll).
- "names" — the full name(s) the posts give for the person the claim is about (not the sources who report it), as an array; empty when unnamed, a group without names, or a minor.
- "place_mention" — the most specific place the post itself names for this happening, even if it is broader or narrower than {{place_scope}}; null if none. Never substitute the scope for what the post said.
- "magnitude" — the numbers the evidence carries, as a small object (a toll, a floor). Hours someone has been trapped are the whole hours since t0 — deriving that is permitted; inventing any other number is not.
- "status" — how it stands now, in a word the text actually supports: atrapado, rescatado, desaparecido, fallecido, en curso, or null. This is independent of "theme": read the outcome, not the operation — "labores de rescate… permanece atrapado" is atrapado, because "rescate" names the effort and not its result. Null is a perfectly good answer, and the right one whenever the post asks rather than tells: a post that names someone, says where they were and asks for news takes null — never desaparecido — unless it says outright that they are missing or unreachable.
- "citations" — the ids that evidence it, most specific and direct first, ties to the earliest time; the first stamps the claim's moment. A post that names the happening only in passing still counts.
