title:: Nothing finds the same person recorded twice
type:: open-question

- **Answered the day it was opened** — see [[decision-2026-07-29-a-machine-may-fold-a-person]]. `Wekui.Narrative.PersonDuplicates` proposes, `Wekui.Pipelines.PersonJudge` decides, `Wekui.Narrative.PersonMerge` performs. The page stays because the question was worth asking and the evidence in it is what shaped the answer.
- **What it was.** A [[place]] recorded twice is found by `Wekui.Gazetteer.Duplicates`. A [[claim]] told twice is found by `Wekui.Narrative.Duplicates`. **A [[person]] recorded twice was found by nothing at all** — and a person is the only one of the three who is a human being in a memorial.
- **It was not hypothetical.** Looking for something else, one woman was found holding **four Person rows**: `belkys josefina barreto garcia`, `belkis josefina barreto garcia`, `belkys barreto`, `belkis barreto`. The event holds 668 Person rows and **zero exact name collisions**, which is exactly why nothing had noticed: every duplicate here is a near-match, and the only check that existed was exact.
- **What it cost.** A person counted twice is counted twice everywhere downstream — in whatever the record eventually says about how many were missing, and in the [[beat]] a reader meets. It also weakened [[decision-2026-07-29-the-record-knows-who]], which uses Person identity to keep two claims apart: two rows for one woman make her two women to any rule that trusts the rows.
- **The three things that looked unsettled, and what settled each.**
  - *A merge of two Persons is heavier than a merge of two Places — may a machine do it at all?* Yes, and the reason was already in the model before the question was asked: a merge here is a **deprecation, never a rewrite**. The folded row keeps its name, says what it became, and its claims keep their link to the name the post actually used. A wrong answer is legible and reversible, and that is the property that makes delegation safe.
  - *A handle already shown to a reader might have to be retired.* The **fullest name survives**, so the surviving handle is the more complete one. This is the opposite of a claim merge on purpose: a claim lives at its first evidence, a person does not live at their first mention.
  - *Names from frightened relatives cannot be trusted as strings.* True, and that is why the deterministic half only ever proposes, and why the deciding half is a judgement about meaning. It is also why the answer had to be spiked three times before it was built.
