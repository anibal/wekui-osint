title:: Research — three independent readers, one taxonomy
type:: research

- The question was whether a vocabulary of [[theme]]s is **in the corpus** or **in the reader**. Three readers were given an identical brief and an identical 302-post sample — a deterministic 1-in-23 slice of the 6,982 Caraballeda posts, exact duplicates removed, spread across all eight days — and each proposed a tree of themes and classified every post. None could see the others. **The comparison is deterministic: no model graded a model.**
- **The structure is in the data.** For every one of the 45,451 post pairs, do two readers both group them or both separate them? Raw agreement is ~90% for all three pairings, but that is inflated because most pairs are apart under any taxonomy. Corrected against what each reader's own density would give by chance: **A↔B 64.1%, A↔C 67.1%, B↔C 58.2%**, where 0% is chance and 100% is identical. Three strangers, no shared words, converging over half the distance to identity.
- **Ten themes were reached by all three**, matched by the posts each covers rather than by the words each chose — four of them covering exactly the same posts to the post. In A's naming: *rescate de animal*, *persona rescatada con vida* (47), *persona fallecida o cuerpo recuperado*, *operación de búsqueda y rescate en curso* (33), *persona atrapada con vida bajo escombros* (61), *solicitud de información* (32), *persona desaparecida o sin contacto* (66), *solicitud de rescate o recursos* (56), *colapso estructural* (32), *sismo o réplica registrada*.
- Seven more were reached by two of three — *apoyo internacional desplegado*, *crítica o denuncia de la respuesta*, *declaración o cifra oficial*, *entrega de ayuda humanitaria*, *búsqueda concluida sin señales de vida*, *mensaje de solidaridad*, *lista o directorio* — and those are the ones worth a person's eye. Where readers differed it was almost always **granularity, not disagreement**: one lumped where another split, most visibly around requests for resources.
- **The corpus insists on a distinction the record does not have.** All three refused to collapse *trapped* (a state) from *a voice heard under the rubble*, *pulled out alive*, and *confirmed dead* (three transitions out of it). Two named it their hardest call, unprompted, and both gave the same reason: the Spanish itself carries the line — *atrapados* / *se escuchan voces* / *lo sacaron con vida*. Our live record holds `búsqueda` and asserts `desaparecido` from a word choice.
- **A plea and a disappearance are two things, and one implies the other.** Reader A settled the hardest boundary this way: a post asking about a **building only** is *solicitud de información* alone; a post asking about a **named person** is **both**, because *asking after someone by name is itself an assertion that their status is unknown*. That is a derivation rule, not a label — and it is the shape the extractor needed and never had.
- **The happening/topic flag survives independent application.** Sixteen of the seventeen themes B and C both found agree on it, and nine of the ten all three found. The single disagreement sits exactly on the state/transition seam: B read *señales de vida* as a moment (happening), A and C read *atrapada con vida* as a condition (topic). They are not quite the same theme, which is why they disagree. **This supports one tree with a flag on the node** rather than two concepts.
  - The honest caveat: the brief supplied the happening/topic distinction, so the flag is not independent of the framing. What IS independent is which concepts each reader put on which side, and that is what agreed.
- **The residue is 2% and it is not a vocabulary gap.** Exactly six of 302 posts were refused by all three readers — and all three refused the SAME six. Read them and they are not un-nameable happenings: they are captions whose evidence is in an image or a video (*"En Caraballeda fue peor: [link]"*, *"Los Corales - Edificio Costa Brava #laguaira [link]"*, *"Esto es en la calle Francisco Fajardo… [link]"*), plus one coordination message and one appeal to a named person. **A text-only pipeline cannot read them at all.** That is a modality gap, and it is a different problem from the one this spike was run to answer.
- **The prior art agrees, and stops one step short.** TREC-IS (NIST) makes `Request-*` versus `Report-*` a first-class branch and carries `Request-InformationWanted` — the same theme v7 invented and all three readers found. HumAID keeps *Requests or urgent needs* separate from *Missing or found people*. But every scheme surveyed is built for **live responder triage**, so `Request-*` names the **speech act**; a memorial wants the **state of the world**. Forty families who ask about one building are not forty requests. Ushahidi, the most deployed of them, has **no fixed taxonomy at all** — categories are per-deployment, which is the same conclusion [[theme]] already reached by scoping to one [[event]].
- **What no corpus of eight days can teach us.** *Vigilia u homenaje a las víctimas* appears **once** in 302 posts, and the prior-art survey found no scheme anywhere with a commemoration category. Both readers who flagged arc-bound themes flagged the same two, and both residue curves are flat across the week — so the vocabulary is stable *within* the first phase. The morph runs on a longer clock than this corpus can show, which is a reason for the vocabulary to be a growable record rather than a fixed list.
- Method notes, so this can be repeated: `scratchpad/brief.md` (the identical brief), `sample.sql`, `compare.exs`, `chance.exs`, `consensus.exs`, `unanimous.exs`. Two of three readers first died at the 64,000-token output ceiling and had to write their answers in several passes — the same wall as [[open-extraction-does-not-batch]], measured a third time. The readers were Sonnet, not the production extractor, so this measures whether a taxonomy EXISTS, not whether the production model can find it.
