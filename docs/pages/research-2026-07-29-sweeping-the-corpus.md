title:: Research — sweeping 2,800 posts, and what the instruments caught
type:: research

- The corpus was read in batches of 25 by `priv/scripts/sweep.exs` until nothing was unread: about 2,800 posts across roughly 120 batches. This is the first measurement of `docs/ontology-fitness.md`'s §3 signals on real volume rather than on one hour of one day.
- **Zero dead vocabulary.** Every one of the 30 active `happening` themes carries at least one claim, and every non-structural `topic` carries at least one post: *solicitud de información* 255 posts, *solicitud de rescate* 183, *solicitud de recursos* 174, *registro visual del daño* 171, *crítica o denuncia* 102, *mensaje de solidaridad* 79, *lista o directorio* 17. The only themes with no use at all are the **six structural hubs**, which say in their own rule that nothing is ever classified against them. A ratified vocabulary of 45 has no unused word in it.
- **The residue rate, and what it meant at each size.** 11.9 entries per 100 posts when the vocabulary was 40 themes in a flat list; 5.4 once the block was grouped by family; and about **1 per 100 after the audit pass**, which is the honest figure because the raw number had stopped measuring gaps. At 17 themes the residue was the corpus asking for words. At 45, **seventeen of eighteen accumulated entries named a happening it already held** — the extractor failing to find them in a longer list.
- **Seven losses, all found by making an instrument say what it was DOING rather than that something had happened. Five of the seven were in instruments built to find losses.**
  - The model copied an id **with its label** — `"id 2071059613336949047"` — and the exact lookup missed. Two whole batches, about **fifty claims**, dropped as `:no_valid_citations`, a reason that is true and useless. The fix is one lenient parser; the diagnosis needed the skip to carry *what* had been cited.
  - The F54 gate refused a `subject` naming a private individual, correctly and absolutely — and a whole happening left the record because one field held a word that belonged in the next field along. **Nine in 375 posts, 2–3% of everything extracted.** Sharpening the prompt fixed it once and it came back; the durable fix is structural, removing from the role the names the model itself declared in `names`. Nothing is lost to a reader: names live on the [[person]] behind the handle gate.
  - A topic that arrived in the `theme` field had its **citations thrown away**, so its posts left no judgment, showed as unread, and were re-read and re-paid for on every sweep.
  - A post the extractor read and said nothing about left **no trace at all** — so it too came back forever. `ThemeNone` had modelled that answer since before the vocabulary existed, at zero rows.
  - Two live-locks: a batch handed the same posts back to itself five times, and another six times. A sweep that cannot account for a batch must set it aside.
  - The refusal-printing added to diagnose all this **crashed the sweep**, because it assumed every skip was `{:no_theme, name}` and a skip can also carry an `Ash.Error`.
- **A pattern worth naming.** [[theme]], `ThemeJudgment` and `ThemeNone` were each built, tested, documented — and each sat at **zero rows** while the same ideas were rebuilt badly in prompts and scripts. The vocabulary of happenings, the record of what a post is about, and the record of having read a post and found nothing were all already designed. Reading the resources before writing the prompt would have saved most of a week.
- **The conservation law, once durable, is the thing that made the rest visible.** Every post now resolves to one of: it evidences a [[claim]], it is about a topic, or it was read and there was nothing in it. Nothing sits outside those three. Two of the seven losses above were invisible precisely because a post could fall out of all of them and nobody counted.
- Evidence: `priv/scripts/sweep.exs` and its residue log; the run receipts; `tmp/` is git-ignored because the residue quotes real posts.
