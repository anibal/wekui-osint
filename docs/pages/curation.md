title:: Curation
type:: concept

- A **curation act** is one deliberate act by a person on the record: who decided, when, what changed, and why. It is the human twin of a [[run]] — a run records *that the system acted*, a curation act records *that a person did* — and it exists for the same reason, so that the record can be audited rather than trusted.
- [[actor]] says that wherever this vocabulary says something was approved, proposed, judged or retired, an Actor did it and we record which one. For an agent the [[run]] says which one. For a person, this does.
- An act names the **person** who decided — an [[actor]] of kind person, never an agent. A machine passed as the curator is refused: the machine's own acts already have a receipt, and an act naming an agent would be that receipt wearing the wrong name.
- An act carries a **reason** — an ordinary sentence, always required. It is the half of the record a later reader cannot reconstruct from the data: the state says *what* is true now, and only the reason says *why a person made it so*.
- An act carries **what the target was** and **what it became**, read at their own moments. They are a display record, not a query surface — the current truth always lives on the thing itself.
- Acts are **append-only**: there is no editing one and no deleting one. A correction of a correction is a second act, and both stay, in order.
  - This is the whole argument against stamping the decider onto the curated thing instead. A stamp answers *who last touched this*; it cannot answer *what did he change that day*, because the second correction overwrites the first. That is [[principle-never-rewrite-the-record]] losing quietly, in the one place the project can least afford it.
- An act is about **exactly one target** — the thing whose state changed: a [[place]], a [[person]], or a [[claim]]. A relink is about the *claim*, because the claim's set of places is what moved, and the place it moved to is named in what it became.
- **An act never travels alone.** The act and the change it attributes are written together or not at all. A change without its act is precisely the unattributed act this concept exists to abolish; an act without its change is a lie.
- The verbs are the ones that already existed — promoting or reparenting or retiring a [[place]], approving or withholding a [[person]], correcting a handle, relinking or retracting a [[claim]]. Curation adds no new ability to the system; it adds the who, the when and the why to abilities it already had.
  - The exception is **accepting a support verdict**: a person reads the gate's flag on a [[claim]] and lets the claim stand. It changes nothing at all, and that is exactly why it must be written down — every other answer moves a status, so its question stops being asked on its own, while this one would be asked forever.
- **One decision is one act, however many rows it moves.** Discovering that a [[place]] was never a distinct place — only another name for one — is a single sentence, so **folding** it is a single act: its children, its names and its claims move onto the Place it turned out to be, and it is deprecated onto that Place. Recording each reparenting on its own would bury the decision under its own consequences; the act counts what moved instead. This is [[decision-2026-07-24-merge-is-deprecation]] said as a verb — there is no merge, because a lifecycle already says *this turned out to be that*.
- **A correction never makes the record forget.** Renaming a [[place]] keeps the name it was called before, marked as the mistake it was: still understood when a [[post]] writes it, never emitted into a query of our own. Forgetting the old spelling would cost us every Post that still uses it, and forgetting is not what a correction is for.
- **One happening is one [[claim]], and the same shape settles it.** Two accounts that may be one happening are offered to a person the way two [[place]]s are: they **merge** — the earlier account survives, because a happening lives at its first evidence, and absorbs the other's evidence and places — or they are **kept apart**, which changes nothing and so lives only in the record. A specific subject carries its own mark across a wrong place; a generic one does not, and there the place must tell them apart.
- A **link between a [[claim]] and a [[place]] may be dropped**, which nothing else in this model permits. A link is a current best reading and not a historical fact — re-resolution already overwrites it — and what it read before survives in the act that changed it, with who changed it and why. The information moves into the audit trail rather than out of the record.
- A curation act can **widen the private-name gate**: promoting a proposed [[place]] adds a name the gate treats as geography, and approving a [[person]] lets their claims be told. That is the sharpest reason attribution is not bookkeeping — the acts with the most weight for dignity are exactly the ones that must say who made them.
- A curator's name is **not** a [[person]]'s name. A Person is whom a [[claim]] is *about*; an Actor is who *acted*. The private-name gate does not reach a curator, and a curator's name never enters a [[claim]] or a [[beat]] — it appears in the audit trail, and in the record of what a human already settled.
- **A person's answer outranks the machine's.** A later [[run]] does not re-decide what a person settled: a [[claim]] a person placed by hand is passed over by the resolver and counted as *settled* in the receipt. Without that rule the next run would quietly undo the act — a place link is an upsert that overwrites how it was resolved, which is right when the resolver corrects itself and wrong when it would correct a person.
- **A ruling is a rule, not a row.** A person is the judgment the system cannot supply, not the labour it will not do — so an answer about one thing is turned into a sweep for everything of its shape, and the sweep is put back in front of them as one question. The operator settling that a parish and the node inside it carrying its name are one place is what taught the system to look for that shape everywhere, and to know the line it must not cross: a *building* named after the area it stands in reduces to the same string and is a real, finer place.
- **A "no" needs an act as much as a "yes" does.** Answering that two look-alike [[place]]s are genuinely different moves no status anywhere, so nothing about either would ever say it was asked and settled. Without an act the question returns on every report — the same shape as accepting a support verdict on a [[claim]].
- The system reads its own acts back: a question a person has already answered stops being asked. Most answers suppress their question by moving the state the question reads; the ones that do not are answered by the act itself.
- Attributing an act does not make it right — it makes it **answerable**. A wrong decision recorded with its reason can be found and reversed by a second act; a wrong decision recorded by nobody cannot ([[principle-a-wrong-answer-is-worse-than-none]]).
- The question this settles is [[open-actor]]; the acts performed before it existed are named in [[decision-2026-07-27-curation-is-a-record]].
