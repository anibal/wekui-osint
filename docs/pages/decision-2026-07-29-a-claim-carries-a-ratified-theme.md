title:: Decision — a claim carries a ratified theme, and a free string carries nothing
type:: decision

- A [[claim]]'s kind is a **[[theme]] a person has taken into the vocabulary**, of nature `happening`. It may carry nothing else: a theme nobody has ratified is refused at the write path, and so is a `topic`, because a post can be *about* a plea while nothing occurred that a claim could assert. `kind` survives as the extractor's own words — provenance, never the vocabulary, nothing filed by it, and no reader ever sees it.
- **What decided it.** `Claim.kind` was an unconstrained string, so **nothing in the system could refuse one**. The consequence was measured on the first real posts the extractor ever read: **twenty of twenty-one support-gate refusals were TYPE errors** — the gate saying *this kind does not apply to this post* — and the extractor had read *"¿alguien tiene información sobre residencias Albatros?"* as *a person is missing*, twenty-three times. It had no word for a family asking, and no rule that could refuse one.
  - The mechanism is the inverse of the obvious reading. The field was not too rigid; it was **too loose to fail**. An open free-text field makes *"this does not fit"* inexpressible — if every string is valid there is no *these* for *"none of these applies"* to refer to — so the model names what it saw with the nearest example it has. **Closing the vocabulary is what creates the possibility of refusal.**
- **What it cost, said plainly.** Binding the claim emptied the memorial: not one of the sixty-seven pre-vocabulary claims could be filed under a ratified happening, and the beat rendered nothing until a themed re-extraction ran. That was the right price. The beat it replaced was twenty-three families who asked, rendered as twenty-three people missing, and `solicitud de información sobre edificio: []` — a field name, a colon and nothing — in public Spanish.
- **Three consumers had each been guessing privately.** The extraction prompt had seven examples and *"there is no fixed list"*; the support prompt had no vocabulary at all and refused kinds that were perfectly valid; the [[beat]] renderer had five regexes and printed `"#{kind}: #{subject}"` when they missed. One vocabulary, written once as a record, is read by all three — and the renderer now dispatches on the theme, so its fallback can no longer print a field name.
- **A ruling that has to hold in two places belongs in neither.** The same failure ran through the gazetteer: the operator's numeral rule lived in `Gazetteer.Duplicates` and never reached `PlaceResolver`, which went on matching *"edificio Celta Mar II"* onto **Torre Celta Mar 1** and asking a person to confirm it. Rules that several consumers need now live in one module both read (`docs/mechanisms.md` is the general form).
- **The vocabulary grows from evidence, not from a desk.** The extractor reports what it cannot name; the residue is clustered, never proposed one entry at a time; a cluster becomes a proposal carrying its own provenance and the model's verbatim words; and a person's word makes it real. Six of the first forty-five themes arrived that way, including two that corrected rules the assistant had written — the area-level collapse a *named-structure* rule was silently discarding, and the nature of *being missing*, which a *"happened at a moment"* definition had excluded and which is the largest theme in the corpus.
- **The residue changes meaning as the vocabulary grows, and must be audited.** At seventeen themes it was the corpus asking for words. At forty-five, **seventeen of eighteen entries named a happening the vocabulary already held** — the extractor failing to find them in a longer list. So an audit pass (`docs/mechanisms.md`, rung four) removes what is already covered before a person reads it, and may only remove: a verdict naming a theme that does not exist covers nothing, an entry it does not rule on stays, and unreadable output suppresses nothing.
- Evidence: [[research-2026-07-27-extraction-on-real-posts]] (the twenty type errors, the seventeen kinds over-split two-to-one), [[research-2026-07-27-three-readers-one-taxonomy]] (the happening/topic line applied identically by three independent readers), `docs/claim-kind-scenarios.md` (the design and the questions it opened), `docs/mechanisms.md` (the ladder).
