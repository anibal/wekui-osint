title:: Decision — the Persons a claim names decide identity before any words do
type:: decision

- Two [[claim]]s that each name a [[person]], and name **different** people, are never one happening. No similarity of wording overrides it, and no model is asked. When only one side names anyone, nothing is decided — absence is not disagreement.
- **What decided it.** Two women of 60 went missing in one building. Both claims read *"una mujer de 60 años"*, the finder scored them 1.0 alike, and **two independent adversarial readings, each instructed to argue DIFFERENT, agreed they were one happening**. The Person rows behind them said `gladismaria pineda ramirez` and `mirta guedez` the whole time. Across the corpus the rule refuses **122 of 3,213 pairs**, five of which the model had upheld — five people who would have been merged away, each with nothing to announce the loss.
- **The mechanism, not the model, is what changed.** The prompt already carried the right sentence — *"a generic subject carries no identity at all"* — with a conditional attached: *without a shared place and a close time*. That conditional is the leak `prompt-craft` names as the anti-pattern, and here the shared place was the same building, so the exception opened and the model walked through it. Deleting an exception from a prompt is not a fix while the same question can be answered from data.
- **The comparison is over words, not over strings.** One woman held **four Person rows** — `belkys josefina barreto garcia`, `belkis josefina barreto garcia`, `belkys barreto`, `belkis barreto`. Whole-string distance reads them 0.70 apart and would have called one woman two, suppressing genuine duplicates; recall over the shorter name's own words reads them 0.944 and up. A dropped middle name is a matter of how much someone wrote down, not of who they meant. The bar is **0.92**, the same the gazetteer settled on for places, and it still refuses `jose luis perez` against `jose luis ramirez` at 0.892.
- **The direction of failure is chosen.** A false *same person* leaves the pair in the queue for a [[curation]] act; a false *different person* removes a genuine duplicate nobody will ever see again. So an ambiguity resolves toward keeping the pair, and `ana maria lopez` against `ana maria lopez de silva` stays a question rather than becoming an answer.
- **This is the third time the record already held what a prompt was guessing.** [[decision-2026-07-29-a-claim-carries-a-ratified-theme]] found [[theme]], `ThemeJudgment` and `ThemeNone` built, tested and sitting at zero rows while the same ideas were rebuilt badly in prompts. `ClaimPerson` was the same: built, green, 554 claims linked, and no mechanism read it.
