title:: Decision — a machine may fold two Person rows, because a merge is a deprecation
type:: decision

- Two [[person]] rows that are one human being are folded by the system itself, with no human gate in front of it. The **fullest name survives**; the folded row keeps its name, carries `merged_into_id` and the reason, and stops being current; every [[claim]] that named it also names the survivor. The act is receipted in a `:person_judge` [[run]] and never as a [[curation]] act — `Wekui.Curation` refuses an agent as curator.
- **What makes it safe is a property that was already there.** A merge in this system is a **deprecation, never a rewrite** ([[decision-2026-07-24-merge-is-deprecation]], [[principle-never-rewrite-the-record]]). Nothing is deleted, so a wrong fold is legible and reversible. The question *"is this too heavy for a machine?"* dissolved once that was noticed: it is heavy only where it is destructive, and here it is not.
  - The claim keeps its link to the name the post actually used. `ClaimPerson` is an immutable upsert, and deleting the old link would erase the true fact that a relative wrote *"Belkis Barreto"*. `PersonMerge.current/1` reads through the chain.
  - The fullest name survives — the OPPOSITE of a claim merge, and deliberately. A claim lives at its **first evidence**; a person does not live at their first mention, so the name with the most parts identifies them best and makes the better handle.
- **The operator's rule about names is in CODE, not in the prompt, and that was measured.** A Venezuelan name is `FIRST NAME · middle · FIRST SURNAME · second surname`; the everyday short form and the full disaster form are one person, because in a catastrophe people write every part to be easier to find. Told that rule in prose — including the literal sentence *"Belkis and Belkys are one name"* — the model split them in **every run**, because the family signal beside it outranked the spelling rule. **Precedence a model must apply in a fixed order to every pair belongs in code**, so a pair position settles never reaches the judge at all.
  - Building it found a defect the prompt had hidden: **a three-word name is ambiguous**. `José Luis Pérez` is a first name, a middle name and one surname; `José Pérez García` is a first name and two surnames. So the rule counts no positions — it asks whether everything the SHORTER name says after the first name is also said by the longer one, and compares de-spaced too, because `Rey Rujano` and `Reyrujan` are one surname (the lesson `Wekui.Gazetteer.Duplicates` learned on *Mc Donalds*).
- **Where a person is still the answer.** The machine folds; it does not decide policy. A fold it declines stays two rows, which is the safe direction — a person wrongly left apart is a redundancy a reader sees through, and two people wrongly merged is one family's relative erased into another's with nothing to announce the loss.
