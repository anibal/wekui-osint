title:: Beat
type:: concept

- A **beat** is the account a reader actually reads: prose written for one [[place]] and one stretch of time, at a chosen grain — a minute, an hour, a day. It is how a person meets the [[claim]]s; the claims are the substance, the beat is their telling.
- A beat is **derived, not authored from scratch.** It is written from the [[claim]]s that hold for its Place and its interval — nothing else — and it can be rebuilt from them at any time. It says nothing its claims do not; it may only word them, connect them, and leave out the lesser ones. This is [[principle-one-name-per-fact]]: the happening lives in the claim, and the beat is a reading of it, never a second copy that could drift.
- A beat's **scope holds by construction.** Written for a [[place]], it draws only the [[claim]]s whose Place lies in that place's subtree, so a beat about one parish cannot wander into another; written for an interval, it draws only the claims evidenced within it.
- A beat sits in a **grain ladder** — minute, hour, day, with wider grains added later as a new rung, not a redesign. A coarser beat is written from the same [[claim]]s over a wider interval, not from the finer beats beneath it: an account is never written from an account, so nothing a lesser telling got wrong can compound upward.
- A beat carries **provenance** like every derived thing here: the [[actor]] who wrote it and the [[claim]]s it rests on. A person editing a beat supersedes it rather than overwriting, the same discipline as a [[judgment]] — [[principle-never-rewrite-the-record]].
- A beat inherits the **person red line** of the [[claim]]s it tells: no private individual is named, ever, whatever the underlying Posts said.
- A beat is **not evidence.** It is a telling of claims, which are themselves accounts drawn from [[post]]s; the evidence is always the Posts, reached through the claims — [[principle-evidence-over-impression]].
