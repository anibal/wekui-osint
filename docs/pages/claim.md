title:: Claim
type:: concept

- A **claim** is an [[actor]]'s account that something happened in an [[event]] — one change on the ground: a building collapsed, a person was rescued, an official toll was announced, aid arrived. Where a [[judgment]] answers a question about a single [[post]], a claim is an account of the world, drawn from many Posts and citing them.
- A claim is not a new kind of judgment; it is a layer above them, built on the same spine. It belongs to one Event; it is append-only and **supersedes**; it carries the [[actor]] who made it and, when that Actor is an agent, a **confidence** — everything [[judgment]] says about provenance, supersession and retraction holds for a claim.
- A claim is **structured**: it holds, each on its own, what happened (its kind — a rescue, a collapse, an official toll, aid arriving; how a claim's kind relates to the What axis of [[theme]]s is a seam still to settle), whom it concerns — by role and attribute, and by an approved [[person]]'s display handle when the evidence names one, never a raw name — **where** it happened (one [[place]]), **when** it was first evidenced, how much (a magnitude, when the evidence carries one — hours trapped, a death toll), and its current **status**. The Spanish a reader sees is written *from* these, never the reverse — that telling is a [[beat]].
- **One claim per happening.** The same rescue reported at one minute and again at noon is one claim, not two; a fresh account of a happening we already hold is folded into the claim it matches, adding its evidence, never a new row. This is [[principle-one-name-per-fact]] for what happened: a happening lives in exactly one claim, and a copy kept elsewhere would drift.
- **Where is a single [[place]], the most specific the evidence supports** — a claim about a building points at the building, not its parish. A claim is answered *for* a coarser Place by asking the Place tree: every claim whose Place lies in a subtree is part of that subtree's account. A parish's story is a reading over its claims, never the same claim copied onto the parish.
- **A claim's evidence is [[post]]s**, and it ships only when they genuinely bear what it asserts, at the strength they show — being cited by a claim is not the same as supporting it. A claim its Posts do not carry is refused, not softened; an uncorroborated point is told as its source's claim, not as fact. This is [[principle-evidence-over-impression]] and [[principle-a-wrong-answer-is-worse-than-none]] at the account layer.
- **A claim shows a person by an approved handle, or by role — never a raw name.** The people a disaster claim concerns — the trapped, the rescued, the missing, the dead — are shown by their [[person]]'s **display handle** (first name and last-name initial, e.g. "Aaron C.") beside the role, and only once a human has approved that person; until then, and whenever the name cannot be resolved, by **role alone**. A raw or unapproved name never appears in a claim. Institutions, officials and other public figures acting publicly may be named as the posts name them; buildings, streets and neighbourhoods are [[place]]s, not people.
- **When two accounts are the same claim** is the second half of [[open-when-two-posts-say-the-same-thing]] — not *is this the same Post twice* (string work) but *are these two people telling us about the same happening*. Two accounts are one claim when their distinguishing marks match, weighed by how specific they are: a specific subject and magnitude — a man of 21, 106 hours — hold across noisy or even wrong [[place]] labels, while a generic subject — a woman — cannot, and there the [[place]] must tell them apart. A woman missing in one building and a woman missing in another are two claims; a rule that merged them on their shared wording would be worse than no rule, by [[principle-a-wrong-answer-is-worse-than-none]].
- **Status threads through supersession.** When the world moves — the missing person is found — the claim's status changes and supersedes its earlier state; it stays one claim, and its history shows the move. A status change is a new answer to the same claim, not a second claim — [[principle-never-rewrite-the-record]].
- **Absent is not zero.** A stretch of time with no claim is a stretch we have not yet found a happening in, not a stretch where nothing happened — [[principle-unknown-is-never-zero]].
- A reader never meets a claim directly; they meet it through a [[beat]], the account written from the claims that hold at a place and a time.
